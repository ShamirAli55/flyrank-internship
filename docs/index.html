Abstract

Can historical search-performance metrics identify content pages at risk of an impressions decline in the following month? Using the FlyRank internship warehouse, a Random Forest classifier was trained using February 2026 historical signals and evaluated using a client-grouped held-out split. The Week-6 audit identified a temporal leakage concern in the original feature construction, and the audited model achieved approximately 42.4% precision, 6.3% recall, and 0.110 F1 on the decline class. The results indicate that the available features provide limited coverage of subsequent declines, although the model can rank a smaller set of pages for review. The resulting queue is therefore treated as directional decision-support, rather than a causal forecast or automatic content recommendation.

1. Introduction

Content teams must regularly decide which pages should be reviewed or refreshed. Without a ranking method, this process can require manual inspection of many pages. The goal of this project is to test whether historical search-performance signals can help prioritize pages for review.

The practical decision supported by the model is not whether a page should definitely be changed. Instead, the output is intended to help an editor decide where limited review time could be spent first.

Research question: To what extent can historical SEO metrics from February 2026 identify content pages that experience a significant decline in organic search impressions in March 2026?

2. Data

The analysis uses the FlyRank/internship-warehouse dataset hosted on Hugging Face.

Tables used:

  • fact_content_daily_performance
  • fact_content_query_90d

February 2026 was used as the feature period and March 2026 as the outcome period. Records with zero or missing previous-month impressions were excluded because the decline calculation requires a positive February value. Rows with missing model features were also removed.

Features

Feature Description
prev_month_impressions Total February impressions for the content page.
visible_queries Number of search queries associated with the content.
rare_share Share of impressions associated with rare queries.
anon_share Share of impressions associated with anonymized queries.
top_query_share Share of impressions concentrated in the top query.

Client and content identifiers were used for grouping and joining only. They were not supplied as model features. Label-derived fields were also excluded from the model inputs.

3. Methodology

Target definition

A page is labelled declining (1) when March impressions are less than 80% of February impressions. Otherwise it is labelled 0. This represents a decline of more than 20%.

Baseline

The Week-4 baseline used a simple visibility-based rule. Pages with at least 1,000 impressions and an average GSC position between 4 and 15 were prioritized.

Model

The model is a Random Forest classifier with 200 estimators, class_weight="balanced", and random state 42.

Validation

A GroupShuffleSplit was used with client_hash_id as the grouping variable. The split used 80% of the data for training and 20% for testing, with random state 42. This prevents the same client from appearing in both the training and test groups.

Leakage audit

The initial Week-5 model produced substantially stronger results. During the Week-6 validation audit, the feature construction was examined for temporal leakage. After restricting the query signals to information available before the outcome period, performance decreased substantially. The audited results below are therefore treated as the more appropriate evidence for the final paper.

4. Results

The audited model was evaluated on the held-out client-grouped test set. The following values are from the audited W06 run.

The audited model has higher precision than the baseline but much lower recall. In practical terms, the model identifies a smaller group of pages with a higher measured proportion of decline cases, but it misses most of the declining pages.

Accuracy should not be treated as the main result because the decline class is a minority class. The precision, recall, and F1 results give a more useful view of the classification behaviour.

Effect of the validation audit

The original Week-5 model reported approximately 0.749 F1 on its evaluation. After the temporal leakage concern was addressed, the audited F1 decreased to approximately 0.110. This large change is an important finding of the project: the original performance should not be presented as evidence of reliable predictive performance.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The model identifies statistical patterns in the available data. It does not explain why a page's impressions decline.
  • The model does not reproduce or predict Google's ranking algorithm.
  • Seasonality, content quality, indexing changes, competitors, and changes in search intent are not fully represented by the features.
  • The experiment uses a February-March 2026 window, so performance on other periods is unknown.
  • The client-grouped test contains a limited number of client groups, so the result should be considered directional.
  • The low recall means the queue should not be treated as a complete list of declining pages.
  • A human reviewer should make the final decision before any content change.
Overall framing: The results are best treated as observed and measured, directional decision-support evidence. They are not a causal claim, production system, or guarantee that refreshing a page will improve its future performance.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The model score is converted into three operational priority levels. These thresholds were selected for the action playbook and are not statistically validated decision boundaries.

Priority Model Score Suggested Action Observed Queue Size
High ≥ 0.70 Review for possible content refresh 76
Medium 0.50 - 0.69 Monitor and consider review 617
Low < 0.50 Low priority / monitor 30,398

The ranked queue is intended to help editors decide where to spend review time first. A high score does not mean that a page definitely needs a refresh. Editors should check the actual search performance, content, search intent, seasonality, and other available context before taking action.

What should not be automated

  • Automatically rewriting or publishing content.
  • Automatically removing pages.
  • Automatically changing titles or search targeting.
  • Automatically treating a model score as proof of a ranking problem.
  • Automatically assuming that a refresh will increase traffic.
